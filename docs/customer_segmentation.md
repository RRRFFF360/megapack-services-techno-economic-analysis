The numerical model motivates these questions. It does not identify customer clusters, measure willingness to pay, or rank providers. Segmentation below is a qualitative decision framework to test through discovery.

## Two dimensions

**Customer service capability (horizontal):** the owner's own ability to execute battery service: qualified personnel, diagnostics, safe work procedures, spare-parts coordination, geographic coverage and escalation. Low means material gaps; high means a mature, suitably qualified service organization. A third-party provider's capability is assessed separately; outsourcing does not make the owner's internal capability high.

**Revenue sensitivity to downtime/performance loss (vertical):** economic exposure to a given outage or performance shortfall. It includes lost contribution margin, contract payments and penalties as applicable, net of avoided costs and recoveries without double counting. High means timing-critical or large exposure; low means smaller/more flexible exposure. These are qualitative discovery labels, not dollar thresholds inferred from DA results. Cost sensitivity is a separate procurement consideration, not the inverse of revenue sensitivity.

Capability narrows feasible delivery options. Revenue exposure raises the required standard of response, restoration, monitoring and accountability under any option. High exposure does not automatically imply Tesla-managed service or prove a price premium is justified.

## Three service delivery models

These are analytical labels for delivery paths, not three official Tesla commercial tiers. Tesla describes two routes: Serviced by Tesla and Certified by Tesla; the latter may involve an in-house or third-party team. [Official Tesla service description](https://www.tesla.com/en_GB/megapack?redirect=no), checked 2026-11-01. Contract terms, certification eligibility and local availability require confirmation.

## Read the map as overlapping options

- Low capability / high exposure: compare Tesla-managed and certified third-party bids against demanding restoration and escalation requirements. OEM preference may favor Tesla-managed; a proven third party may still qualify.
- Low capability / lower exposure: compare both outsourced routes on total cost and sufficient coverage. Lower exposure does not relax safety or qualification requirements.
- High capability / high exposure: certified in-house is feasible if coverage and escalation are adequate; outsourcing remains an option. Examine critical spares and contract risk.
- High capability / lower exposure: in-house may fit a control-focused owner; compare its fully loaded cost against outsourcing. No option is automatically optimal.

The figure deliberately shows overlapping outsourced options and in-house service spanning revenue exposure levels. Marker placement is illustrative, not a customer dataset, market share or provider-performance score.

## Product recommendations and validation

1. Add a discovery worksheet covering team readiness, site coverage, restoration targets, contract exposure and service budget. Do not infer a customer's needs solely from company type.
2. Compare three delivery options using matched scope and fully loaded lifecycle cost: fees, internal labor/training, provider management, spares, exclusions, replacement exposure and expected outage loss. Confirm warranty and responsibility boundaries from actual contracts; do not assume certification makes coverage identical.
3. For high-exposure customers, establish requirements for diagnosis, response, restoration, monitoring, parts and escalation regardless of provider. These are proposed evaluation criteria, not promised Tesla SLAs or available add-on packages.
4. Interview owners across capability/exposure combinations and solicit comparable provider quotes. Check whether preferences match the hypotheses and revise the map if they do not. Validate a third-party cost advantage only when matched bids and risk-adjusted total costs support it.

No customer interviews, third-party price comparisons, service-specific failure-rate data, or willingness-to-pay estimates have been collected in this study. Therefore no numerical service price, savings claim or causal provider ranking is recommended.
